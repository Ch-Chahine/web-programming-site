History of the Internet

Internet History Timeline

1969 — ARPANET

ARPANET was developed by DARPA in 1969 and first connected four computers. On October 29, the first computer-to-computer message was sent from UCLA to SRI. ARPANET became an important starting point for the development of the Internet.

DARPA — ARPANET

1971 — Networked Email

In 1971, Ray Tomlinson sent the first networked email using ARPANET. This gave people a new way to send messages between computers connected to a network. Email later became one of the most common ways people communicate over the Internet.

Computer History Museum — Ray Tomlinson PDF

1973 — International ARPANET Connections

In 1973, ARPANET expanded outside the United States through connections to Norway and the United Kingdom. This was an important step because ARPANET was becoming an international network.

Computer History Museum — Internet History

1973–1974 — Development of TCP

In 1973, Bob Kahn and Vint Cerf worked on a protocol that could connect different computer networks. Their work was published in 1974 and later developed into TCP/IP. This helped make communication between different networks possible.

Computer History Museum — Internet History

1983 — ARPANET Transition to TCP/IP

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET changed from NCP to TCP/IP. TCP/IP gave computers a common set of rules for communicating with each other. This change was an important step toward the modern Internet.

Internet Society — Internet History

1983–1984 — DNS Introduction and Development

In 1983, Paul Mockapetris published the documents that defined DNS. In 1984, top-level domains such as .com, .edu, .gov, .mil and .org were introduced. DNS made the Internet easier to use because people could use domain names instead of only numerical addresses.

Internet Society — DNS History

1986 — The Launch of NSFNET

In 1986, the National Science Foundation launched NSFNET to connect researchers with supercomputer centers. NSFNET helped more researchers access computer networks and later became an important Internet backbone in the United States. This helped the Internet continue to grow.

National Science Foundation — Internet

1990 — ARPANET Was Decommissioned

In 1990, the original ARPANET was officially shut down. By this time, the Internet had already grown into a much larger network connecting many different networks. ARPANET had played an important role in making this development possible.

DARPA — ARPANET

1995 — NSFNET Backbone Was Retired

In 1995, the NSFNET backbone was retired as commercial Internet services continued to grow. This marked an important change from a government-supported backbone to more privately operated Internet networks.

National Science Foundation — Internet

2000s — Broadband and Wi-Fi Growth

During the 2000s, broadband and Wi-Fi became much more common. Broadband gave people faster Internet connections, while Wi-Fi allowed devices to connect without cables. Together, they made Internet access faster and more convenient.

Pew Research Center — Broadband Pew Research Center — Wi-Fi

2000s–2010s — Mobile Internet Growth

During the 2000s and 2010s, mobile Internet grew quickly. More people were able to connect to the Internet using mobile devices instead of depending only on fixed connections. This made Internet access available in more places.

ITU — ICT Facts and Figures 2015

2010s–2020s — Expansion of Fiber, 4G and 5G

During the 2010s and 2020s, fiber broadband and mobile networks such as 4G and 5G expanded. These technologies provided faster Internet speeds and better network capacity. They also helped high-speed Internet become available to more people.

OECD — Fiber ITU — Mobile Network

Key Internet Technologies

Packet Switching

Packet switching breaks data into smaller pieces called packets before sending them through a network. The packets can travel through different paths and are put back together when they reach their destination. This became an important technology for ARPANET and the Internet.

DARPA — ARPANET

TCP/IP

TCP/IP is a set of rules that allows computers and different networks to communicate with each other. It was important because different networks could use the same communication rules. ARPANET started using TCP/IP in 1983.

Internet Society — Internet History

DNS

DNS connects domain names to the numerical IP addresses used by computers. This means people can use easy-to-remember names instead of remembering IP addresses. DNS made the Internet easier to organize and use.

Internet Society — DNS History
